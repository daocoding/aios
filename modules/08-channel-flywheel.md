# 08 · Channel flywheel

**Purpose:** don't judge a creator-led push by one dashboard. Design the full path from attention to profit.

```
Creator & short-video platforms ──ignite──▶ Marketplaces ──capture──▶ Owned store / list ──compound──▶ Brand moat
        (cheapest attention)             (spillover search)          (margin, retention, repeat)
```

1. **Ignite:** creator platforms are where attention is cheapest today. Win there first.
2. **Capture:** people who saw you in a video often search your brand on the marketplace they already trust. Watch branded search and reviews that mention the video.
3. **Compound:** an owned store or email list holds higher-margin products, repeat purchases, direct reach, and less platform risk. Point profile links there.

## What to measure
- Branded search volume and marketplace sales after content pushes (look for the lag)
- Owned-channel signups and repeat rate
- Blended customer acquisition cost across channels, not per channel (aiOS addition; the lecture judges total business lift)

## Decision rules
- If a video push lifts marketplace sales, credit the content when deciding budgets.
- For repeat-purchase products, prioritize building the owned channel early.
- Treat every new region or channel as a new hypothesis (module 00).

Platform windows let you win once. The method lets you keep winning when the market gets competitive. AI makes the method durable.

## Field notes (v2)
- Engine (creator platforms) → base (marketplaces) → profit pool (owned store/community) → moat (brand). Judge total business lift, not one dashboard.
- Proof case (lecture): a marketplace seller took ~8 years to reach ≈$6.4M a year; a newcomer took the short-video channel to ≈$7.1M in 18 months and lifted the marketplace by nearly the same amount, to ≈$14M, almost all through brand-name search.
