Effort isn't the bottleneck. Decision quality is.

Most creator-led growth stalls for the same reason: teams work hard on problems that were never defined, and spend money without learning what went wrong. aiOS makes the difference between two kinds of operator explicit, then hands the verified path to your agents.

The method, in four ideas

The full theory, with principles, six traps, frameworks, and decision rules, lives in METHODOLOGY.md. Here's the shape of it.

Value, not cost

Price from the pain, not the factory

Cost sets your floor and value sets your ceiling. Score every offer on three axes: how expensive the pain is, the true margin after every fee, and whether the core difference can be shown in 20 seconds.

One variable

Test small, test single

Hold everything fixed except the one thing you're learning about. Write the kill criterion before you spend. Run a 30-day sprint, and commit only after engagement, creator feedback, and real purchases all agree.

Assets, not posts

Creators teach you what sells

The first creator wave buys learning and reusable assets, not sales. Every winning piece is broken down into hook, proof, scene, and call to action, then recombined and remade until it stops working.

Humans decide

No SOP, no agent

Humans set standards and make decisions. Agents run the process and keep the learning. Anything you can write down, an agent can run, so capability belongs to the company, not to one person.

Three-Beat Resonance: the structure of a short video that sells

Every beat is required, they always come in this order, and each video makes one selling point.

0–3s

Stop

A relatable hook or rising pain that stops the scroll.

3–11s

Believe

Demo, proof, before and after, in the real scene where it's used, with credible social proof.

11–15s

Act

The offer, a reason to act now, and a clear instruction.

Paid growth has stages, and each one has its own rules

0 → 1 · Validate

Let the system learn who buys. Aim for about 50 conversions and don't judge the project on early ROI.

1 → 10 · Replicate

Build a matrix of creatives. Put budget behind proven performers, and keep the awareness-building videos running.

10 → 100 · Scale

Run paid as a shared service. Scale comes from the number of proven creatives, not just the budget.

The human approval step, built in

Your agents put every post, video script, and creator message into a review queue. You see one compact, full-width row per item. Select all, bulk-approve, edit inline, or send something back with a comment. Your agent publishes only what you approved.

Agent drafts→Queue→You approve, edit, or send back→Agent publishes→Results logged→Hypothesis updated

Your account, your data

Deploy the queue to your own Cloudflare account (Pages + Functions + D1, free tier friendly). aios.pages.dev is the project's home and hosted version.

Zero-dependency option

Prefer to run it locally? The Python version uses only the standard library and SQLite, so it runs on any laptop or small server.

Fifteen decision rules to keep on your desk

  1. Don't decide until the real problem is written as a cause-and-effect claim you can test.
  2. Test one variable at a time. Write the kill criterion before you spend.
  3. Don't make a large commitment on fewer than three independent signals.
  4. Don't make directional decisions at an emotional high or low.
  5. Every new market, audience, or channel is a new hypothesis.
  6. If the value gap is large, prioritize the offer. If it's moderate, use it to bring in traffic. If it's negative, drop it.
  7. Start with a small batch and a 30-day sprint. Scale only after the content converts.
  8. If fewer than 90% of creators who get samples post content, fix who you pick or your terms first.
  9. One selling point per video, always in the order Stop → Believe → Act.
  10. In early paid tests, optimize for learning, not ROI.
  11. When results are below break-even, check in order: target → activation → creative → page.
  12. Put budget behind proven performers, and keep awareness-building videos running.
  13. Keep remaking a winner until it stops working, not until you're bored of it.
  14. Every plan survives three "why?"s and includes a Plan B and a Plan C.
  15. If you can't write the SOP, you can't hand the job to an agent.
